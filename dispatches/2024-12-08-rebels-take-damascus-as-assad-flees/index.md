# Rebels Take Damascus as Bashar al-Assad Flees

*An offensive led by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham reaches the capital in 11 days and ends 54 years of Assad family rule.*

Rebels led by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham took Damascus on 8 December 2024, eleven days after they opened their offensive. Bashar al-Assad flew to Moscow.

His family had ruled Syria since 1970.

![ΛRX Intercept dispatch card (Voice of America photo): Syrians wave the opposition flag at the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus on 14 December 2024](card.png)

Hayat Tahrir al-Sham and allied factions opened their offensive from Idlib on 27 November 2024. They took Aleppo by 30 November, Hama on 5 December and Homs on 7 December as government forces pulled back. Southern rebel groups closed on Damascus, and on 8 December the opposition declared victory on state television. Bashar al-Assad flew out through Russia's Hmeimim air base to Moscow, where Russia granted him asylum. His father, Hafez al-Assad, had seized power in 1970, and Bashar had ruled since 2000.

HTS leader Abu Mohammed al-Jolani, who now uses his real name, Ahmed al-Sharaa, spoke at the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus that day. Benjamin Netanyahu said the 1974 disengagement agreement had collapsed with the Syrian army's retreat and ordered Israeli troops into the buffer zone below the Golan Heights. Al-Sharaa led the caretaker government and became Syria's president on 29 January 2025. The collapse cut Iran's land route to Hezbollah in Lebanon and put Russia's bases at Hmeimim and Tartus in doubt.

Al-Sharaa says "a new history, my brothers, is being written in the entire region after this great victory." Joe Biden says "the fall of the regime is a fundamental act of justice." Iran's Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei says "what happened in Syria was the product of a joint American and Zionist plot." Assad says from Moscow that "at no point during these events did I consider stepping down or seeking refuge."

Assad's foes call his fall justice for the Syrians his government jailed and killed; his camp calls it a plot from abroad. Beneath both is a contest over Syria's alignment: whether Damascus stays the corridor for Iran's reach into Lebanon and Russia's foothold on the Mediterranean, or answers to new patrons.
